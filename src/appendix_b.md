# Appendix B

## Additional Resources for Chapter 1:

**PAPERS:**

- **[Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)**: Satoshi's nine pages that started everything. Shorter than you would expect.

- **[b money](https://www.weidai.com/bmoney.txt)**: Wei Dai's original proposal, the one that never got implemented but named the movement's money.

- **[Bit Gold](https://unenumerated.blogspot.com/2005/12/bit-gold.html)**: Nick Szabo's unimplemented sketch of decentralized digital scarcity, from his own blog.

- **How to Time-Stamp a Digital Document** by Stuart Haber and W. Scott Stornetta: the 1991 paper behind the "chain of hashed documents" idea.

- **Blind Signatures for Untraceable Payments** by David Chaum: the cryptography behind eCash.

**ONLINE:**

- **[Nakamoto Institute Literature](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/literature/)**: a well-organized archive of the early literature, from Hashcash to Secure Property Titles.

**BOOKS:**

- **"Digital Gold"** by Nathaniel Popper

    - The story of Bitcoin's strange early years, told like a novel.

- **"The Internet of Money"** by Andreas M. Antonopoulos

    - Talks about why decentralized money matters, not how it works.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 2:

**MANIFESTOS:**

- **[A Cypherpunk's Manifesto](https://www.activism.net/cypherpunk/manifesto.html)** by Eric Hughes: the "Cypherpunks write code" text quoted in the chapter.

- **[The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto](https://www.activism.net/cypherpunk/crypto-anarchy.html)** by Timothy C. May: the 1988 text where the whole idea of an untraceable, stateless economy was laid out first.

**ONLINE:**

- **[Cypherpunk Mailing List Archive](https://cypherpunks.venona.com/)**: decades of the actual conversations where these ideas were argued into existence.

**BOOKS:**

- **"Cypherpunks: Freedom and the Future of the Internet"** by Julian Assange, Jacob Appelbaum, Andy Müller-Maguhn, and Jérémie Zimmermann

    - A round-table style look at where the movement thought it was going.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 3:

**BOOKS:**

- **"Crypto: How the Code Rebels Beat the Government, Saving Privacy in the Digital Age"** by Steven Levy

    - The full story of the Crypto Wars, from public-key cryptography to the Clipper Chip.

- **"The Code Book"** by Simon Singh

    - Cool history of cryptography and its development over time.

- **"Code: And Other Laws of Cyberspace"** by Lawrence Lessig

    - The book that made "code is law" a serious legal argument.

**ESSAYS:**

- **[Why I Wrote PGP](https://www.philzimmermann.com/EN/essays/WhyIWrotePGP.html)** by Phil Zimmermann: the author's own account of the export control fight.

- **"The Moral Character of Cryptographic Work"** by Phillip Rogaway: a long academic essay on the politics hiding inside cryptography.

**ONLINE:**

- **[The Tor Project](https://www.torproject.org/)**: documentation and research behind the anonymity network mentioned throughout the chapter.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 4:

**BOOKS:**

- **"Mastering Bitcoin"** by Andreas M. Antonopoulos

    - The technical deep dive into how Bitcoin actually works inside.

    - Free and open source at the [official GitHub repository](https://github.com/bitcoinbook/bitcoinbook).

- **"The Basics of Bitcoins and Blockchains"** by Antony Lewis

    - A gentle but honest walkthrough of the core mechanisms.

**SPECS & STANDARDS:**

- **[RFC 6979 - Deterministic Usage of DSA and ECDSA](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc6979)**: how deterministic nonces made signature schemes safe from their worst failure mode.

- **[RFC 8032 - Ed25519 / EdDSA](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8032)**: the specification of the signature scheme used by the newer chains.

- **[RFC 7748 - Curve25519 and X25519](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc7748)**: the elliptic curves behind modern key exchange.

- **[The Byzantine Generals Problem](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/byz.pdf)** by Lamport, Shostak, and Pease: the 1982 paper that framed the consensus problem.

**ONLINE:**

- **[Bitcoin P2P Network Reference](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/p2p_networking.html)**: how Bitcoin's gossip and discovery actually behave on the wire.

- **[libp2p Documentation](https://docs.libp2p.io/)**: the modular networking stack used by Ethereum 2, Filecoin, Polkadot, and more.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 5:

**PAPERS & SPECS:**

- **[Ethereum Whitepaper](https://ethereum.org/en/whitepaper/)**: Vitalik's original introduction to the world computer idea.

- **[Ethereum Yellow Paper](https://ethereum.github.io/yellowpaper/paper.pdf)**: the formal specification of the protocol and the EVM. Not a light read.

- **[EIP-1559: Fee market change](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559)**: the specification of the base fee and burning mechanism.

**BOOKS:**

- **"Mastering Ethereum"** by Andreas M. Antonopoulos and Gavin Wood

    - Covers the EVM, smart contracts, and accounts in real depth.

    - Free and open source at the [official GitHub repository](https://github.com/ethereumbook/ethereumbook).

**ONLINE:**

- **[Solidity Documentation](https://docs.soliditylang.org/)**: the language the contracts are written in.

- **[EVM Opcodes Reference](https://www.evm.codes/)**: every opcode with its gas cost, a great place to see the price list yourself.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 6:

**BOOKS:**

- **"Tracers in the Dark"** by Andy Greenberg

    - Real investigations of how chain analysis actually de-anonymized people. Scary and well told.

- **"Mastering Monero"** by Justin Ehrenhofer and the Monero community

    - Privacy theory plus practical Monero usage, from the community itself.

    - Freely available at [masteringmonero.com](https://masteringmonero.com/).

**ONLINE:**

- **[Monero Official Resources](https://www.getmonero.org/)**: documentation, research papers, and the [library section](https://www.getmonero.org/library/) with the technical "Zero to Monero" book.

- **[Zcash](https://z.cash/)**: documentation of the shielded pool and zk-SNARK usage in practice.

- **[Vitalik's Blog](https://vitalik.ca/)**: his posts on privacy and ZK-Proofs are the best non-academic explanations around.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 7:

**BOOKS:**

- **"Radical Markets"** by Eric A. Posner and E. Glen Weyl

    - The origin of quadratic voting and a lot of mechanism design ideas worth stealing.

**PAPERS:**

- **[The Byzantine Generals Problem](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/byz.pdf)**: also belongs here, since governance is consensus plus politics.

**ONLINE:**

- **[DeepDAO](https://deepdao.io/)**: analytics on real DAO treasuries, participation, and whales. Good reality check.

- **[Snapshot](https://snapshot.org/)**: the off-chain voting platform many DAOs actually run on.

- **[Aragon](https://aragon.org/)**: tooling for building and governing DAOs on-chain.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 8:

**BOOKS:**

- **"Algorithmic Game Theory"** edited by Noam Nisan, Tim Roughgarden, Éva Tardos, and Vijay Vazirani

    - The formal toolkit behind mechanism design and incentive analysis.

- **"Networks, Crowds, and Markets"** by David Easley and Jon Kleinberg

    - How incentives and network structure interact, with almost no prerequisites.

    - A free complete preprint is hosted on [Cornell's page](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/networks-book/).

**ONLINE:**

- **[Flashbots Documentation](https://docs.flashbots.net/)**: the MEV supply chain explained by the people who mapped it.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 9:

**SPECS & STANDARDS:**

- **[BIP 39 - Mnemonic code for generating deterministic keys](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0039.mediawiki)**: the seed phrase standard your wallet follows.

- **[BIP 32 - Hierarchical Deterministic Wallets](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0032.mediawiki)**: how one seed derives an entire tree of keys.

**ONLINE:**

- **[rekt.news](https://rekt.news/)**: post-mortems of every major exploit, written like crime reports. Best way to learn what actually goes wrong.

- **[Ethereum is a Dark Forest](https://www.paradigm.xyz/2020/08/ethereum-is-a-dark-forest)** by Robinson and Konstantopoulos: the story that made MEV visible to everyone.

- **[Escaping the Dark Forest](https://www.paradigm.xyz/2020/08/escaping-the-dark-forest)**: the sequel, where an actual rescue race happens in real time.

## Additional Resources for Chapter 10:

**SPECS & STANDARDS:**

- **[W3C Decentralized Identifiers](https://www.w3.org/TR/did-core/)**: the standard behind DID claims in the chapter.

**ONLINE:**

- **[IPFS Documentation](https://docs.ipfs.tech/)**: content addressing and how files survive without servers.

- **[Filecoin](https://filecoin.io/)**: the incentive layer built on top of that storage idea.

- **[Join Mastodon](https://joinmastodon.org/)**: the easiest entry point into the fediverse mentioned in the chapter.

- **[The Network State](https://thenetworkstate.com/)**: Balaji Srinivasan's speculative book on network-first societies, free online. Read it as a thought experiment, not a forecast.

**BOOKS:**

- **"The Sovereign Individual"** by James Dale Davidson and William Rees-Mogg

    - A 1997 prediction book that crypto people keep quoting. Some of it aged well, some of it did not, which is exactly why it is interesting.
